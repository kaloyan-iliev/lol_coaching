## Jungle Fundamentals

### Core Principles
Jungling revolves around three interconnected principles that form the foundation of effective gameplay:
- **Tempo**: How you use your time on the map (clear speed, base timing, camp management)
- **Information Gathering**: How you collect and process data (minimap awareness, chat, tracking enemy jungler)
- **Execution**: How you convert information into action (ganks, fights, objective control)

These principles work together: tempo creates opportunities, information gathering identifies which opportunities to take, and execution converts them into advantages.

### Tempo Management
Tempo is the jungler's most critical resource—it dictates your ability to control the map and create advantages.

**Clear Speed Optimization**
- Pull camps using auto attacks to move them toward your next camp (every auto attack should be pulling the camp)
- Avoid kiting toward your next camp—this wastes significant time and hurts tempo
- Clear speed differences between ranks often come from these micro-optimizations, not just champion choice
- Example: Proper camp pulling can save 20-25 seconds per clear compared to walking between camps

**Camps Up vs. Camps Down Framework**
- **Camps Up** (your camps are alive): Only take plays you're 90%+ certain to succeed
  * Ganks, invades, or objectives must feel like a "guaranteed kill" or near-certain outcome
  * If uncertain, default to farming your camps
- **Camps Down** (after completing your clear): This is "free time" for lower-percentage plays
  * Use this window for vision control, lane pressure, preparing for next objective, or scouting
  * Example: After clearing Krugs, you have time to check mid lane for summoner spells before deciding on your next move

**Base Timing Efficiency**
- Base only when it's efficient—when you have nothing else to do on the map
- Value of basing: 8 seconds of basing is worth it when you have ~1000 gold, but not when you have less than 500 gold
- One-shot camps with Smite when recalling (don't hold Smite unnecessarily)
- Inefficient basing example: Waiting 3 seconds at Krugs when you should be moving costs meaningful tempo over multiple clears

**Tempo Matching**
- Constantly compare your tempo to the enemy jungler's position and pathing
- Aim to match their clearing speed—if they're ahead, adjust your path to contest objectives; if behind, invade or take opposite-side objectives
- Your "tempo line" (where you are on the map vs. where the enemy jungler is) should remain balanced

### Information Gathering
Effective jungling requires treating information as your primary weapon—70% of your decisions should come from minimap awareness.

**Minimap Awareness**
- Prioritize minimap prompts: flashing icons, dropping health bars, summoner spell cooldown timers
- When you see a prompt, immediately move your camera to investigate (e.g., enemy mid laner flashing = potential gank opportunity)
- This is especially critical in jungle since you can influence all lanes

**Camera Panning (Click and Pan)**
- Click on a camp and pan your camera to nearby lanes while auto-running between camps
- Priority: Pan to lanes you're closest to (where you're in proximity)
- Be intentional: Before panning, ask yourself "What am I trying to learn?" (e.g., "What will I do after my blue buff?")
- Example: After clearing your blue buff, click Gromp and pan top lane to see if enemy top laner has flash

**Chat Awareness for Summoner Spells**
- Focus exclusively on summoner spell information in chat (especially flash status)
- Mute non-essential chat, keep party chat only for critical spell information
- Enemy having no flash is significant information—it creates gank opportunities that wouldn't exist otherwise
- Track key spells: Flash, Heal, Barrier, Ultimates (these change fight outcomes dramatically)

**Jungle Tracking and Pathing**
- Download enemy jungler's pathing by:
  * Placing wards on their jungle camps
  * Observing when they first appear on the map (e.g., seeing them at Raptors reveals their start)
  * Using process of elimination (if they're not where expected, they must be elsewhere)
- Know their exact position to compare tempo and judge threats
- Use this information to:
  * Ping laners about incoming ganks ("Enemy jungler is on your side—play safe")
  * Decide when to invade (if they're bot side, you can safely take top side)
  * Predict objective timings (if they're taking dragon, you can take herald or invade)

**Mental Stack Management**
- Avoid distractions (Discord, screen sharing with friends) that consume mental processing power
- Plan item builds before the game—not during—to free up cognitive resources
- An empty mental stack allows you to process map information faster and make better decisions
- Example: Knowing your build before loading in lets you focus entirely on minimap awareness during early game

### Decision-Making Frameworks
Apply these frameworks consistently to convert information into advantages.

**Match Trade Minimize**
- **Match when stronger**: When you have numbers advantage, ults, items advantage, or level lead
  * Example: Taking 3v3 bot lane when your laners have ults and enemy doesn't
- **Trade when weaker**: Exchange resources of equivalent value
  * Example: Taking enemy red side when they're taking dragon (trading jungle pressure for objective)
- **Minimize when on same side**: When you and enemy jungler are on the same map half
  * Just give the objective and take your camps—don't overcommit to unfavorable fights
  * Example: Enemy is taking bot scuttle; instead of contesting, take your top side camps

**Guaranteed Plays vs. Percentage Plays**
- Only take plays you're 90%+ certain of when your camps are up
- When camps are down (free time), you can explore lower-percentage plays (60-70% certainty)
- After any play, immediately ask: "What can the enemy jungler do?" and ping their possible locations
- Always assess and plan your next move after your last camp before deciding to recall or make another play

**When in Doubt, Farm**
- If uncertain about an invade, gank, or objective contest, default to farming your camps
- Invading without certainty often leads to tempo deaths that hurt more than the potential gain
- Farming is never a bad play when information is incomplete

### Jungle Economy and XP/Gold Sources
Efficient resource management separates high-elo junglers from low-elo ones.

**Camp Clearing Efficiency**
- Pulling camps (not walking between them) is the full distance) saves 15-25 seconds per clear
- Proper leashing: Use auto attacks to pull camps as you move, then Smite at the end
- Example: Pulling Gromp toward your next camp while auto-attacking saves 3-4 seconds per camp

**Objective Prioritization**
- Priority order: Your camps > Rift Herald/Scuttle > Enemy jungle > Lane pressure
- Always secure your own camps before seeking external objectives
- When enemy jungler is occupied (e.g., taking dragon), you have a window to:
  * Take opposite-side objectives (if they're bot dragon, take top herald)
  * Invade their jungle
  * Prepare for counter-gank

**Spare Time Utilization**
- After full clear, use spare time for:
  * Sweeping lanes for vision
  * Checking lane summoner spells (especially flash)
  * Preparing for next objective (warding dragon/herald pit)
  * Looking for low-percentage plays only when camps are down
- Example: After clearing Krugs, spend 10 seconds sweeping mid lane for ward placements before deciding on next camp

**Gold and XP Efficiency**
- Base timing directly affects gold/XP income—basing too early/late loses meaningful resources
- Every second spent inefficiently walking between camps is lost XP and gold
- Controlling objectives (crabs, herald, dragon) provides burst gold/XP that can reset tempo deficits

### Role Identity and Mental Approach
Your mindset and role understanding determine how effectively you apply technical skills.

**Role Identity**
- For carry junglers (Assassins, Fighters): Primary focus should be on how to get fed; everything else is secondary
- For utility junglers (Tanks, Supports): Focus on enabling lanes and controlling objectives
- Regardless of role, your first priority is always efficient jungle clearing and tempo maintenance

**Mental Framework**
- Treat the game like an exam with 50 questions: Focus on consistent correct decisions rather than win/loss
  * Mathematically, your rank is determined by your average performance over 100+ games
  * Hitting 40/50 correctly consistently will climb you regardless of occasional losses
- Don't let bad games (15% losses) affect your performance in winnable games (70%)
  * One bad game shouldn't change your approach to the next 6-7 games
- Be curiosity-driven, not pain-driven, when reviewing losses
  * Ask "What can I improve?" (tempo, trades, teamfights, mechanics) not "Why is my team bad?"
- When you die, immediately think "What's next?" not "What did I do wrong?"
  * This prevents emotional decision-making and keeps you focused on future opportunities
- Lock in: Eliminate distractions to maintain full mental presence on the game state
  * Your ability to process minimap information and make decisions depends on focused attention

### Common Mistakes
- **Indecision**: The #1 problem is not making a decision—not the specific choice you make
- **Poor base timing**: Basing when inefficient (e.g., with <500 gold value) instead of waiting for optimal moment
- **Not pulling camps**: Walking full distance between camps instead of using auto attacks to pull them
- **Kiting toward next camp**: Moving toward your next camp without pulling the current camp (wastes 3-5 seconds per camp)
- **Ignoring minimap prompts**: Missing critical information like flashing icons or dropping health bars
- **Not tracking enemy jungler**: Playing blind to enemy movements and missing invasion/counter-gank opportunities
- **Overforcing when behind**: Trying to make high-percentage plays when you lack advantage (leads to deaths)
- **Not using spare time effectively**: Standing idle after full clear instead of warding, checking lanes, or preparing
- **Holding Smite unnecessarily**: Not one-shotting camps when recalling (wastes 2-3 seconds per clear)
- **Distractions during game**: Using Discord or screen sharing, which reduces mental processing power by 30-50%
- **Blaming teammates/"losers queue"**: Focusing on external factors instead of self-improvement and decision quality
- **Non-objective decision-making**: Letting emotions ("I need to help this lane") dictate plays instead of analyzing advantage states
- **Gathering but not processing information**: Seeing minimap prompts but failing to act on the information (e.g., noticing enemy has no flash but not ganking)

## Clearing & Camp Mechanics

### Core Clearing Techniques
- Pull camps during every auto attack: Direction your movement toward the next camp while attacking (e.g., while hitting Gromp, pull it along your path to Wolves)
- Kiting toward next camp: Instead of walking straight to camps, guide them in your travel direction to eliminate transition time between camps
- Optimize camp completion points: Finish each camp at a location that positions you immediately for the next camp (e.g., end Gromp where you can walk straight to Wolves without repositioning)
- Utilize full leash range: Maximize camp pull distance while moving to minimize idle walking between auto attacks
- Champion-specific optimization: Even slow-clearing champions (like Jax) must mechanics-minmax their clear through precise pulling and kiting

### Tempo Management Framework
- **Camps Up vs Camps Down** (universal coach consensus):
  - When YOUR camps are alive (up): Only execute actions with ≥90% success certainty (guaranteed kills, safe objectives, high-percentage ganks)
  - When YOUR camps are dead (down): Free time for information gathering, invasive plays, or lower-percentage opportunities
- Base timing: Recall immediately after full clear to reset timers and avoid walking back wasted time
- Spare time allocation: Use camps-down periods for:
  - Sweeping lanes for vision control
  - Checking enemy jungler position via mini map/chat
  - Low-risk counter-jungling when enemy location known
  - Objective control only when vision-secured and camp timers allow

### Information Gathering During Clear
- Click and pan technique: When moving between camps, click your destination and pan camera toward nearby lanes (prioritizing closest lanes) to gather info without losing clear speed
- Intentional camera panning: Before panning, define exactly what information you need (e.g., "Where is enemy jungler after my blue?")
- Mini map discipline: Continuously track enemy jungler icons, health bars, and lane states during clear
- Chat monitoring: Use chat history (V key bind) to track enemy summoner spell usage and pings for decision-making
- Lane proximity priority: When panning, focus first on lanes nearest to your current path (e.g., bot lane when between Krugs and Raptors)

### Ability Usage & Clear Optimization
- Ability efficiency: Avoid wasting abilities on camps when they could save time (e.g., holding smite for objectives unless camp clear is threatened)
- Pathing awareness: While moving between camps, maintain awareness of enemy jungler's likely position based on tracked pathing
- Post-last camp planning: Before finishing your final camp (Gromp/Krugs/etc.), already know your next action (recall, gank, invade) based on camp state and enemy position
- Base timer precision: Time recalls to arrive at camps exactly as they spawn (e.g., be at Gromp 0.5 seconds before spawn)

### Common Mistakes
- Walking between camps without pulling or kiting (wasting 15-25 seconds per full clear)
- Failing to kite camps toward next destination, repositioning camp leashiting (losing 15-25 seconds per clear)
- Ignoring camp leash range mechanics and walking camps in place
- Ganking or invading when your own camps are up (causing tempo loss)
- Not tracking enemy jungler path during clear, leading to surprise invades
- Wasting camps-down time on indecision instead of information gathering or safe plays
- Taking dragon without vision control when it risks losing multiple camps
- Failing to plan next move after last camp before recalling (causing idle time)
- Arriving late to camp spawns due to poor base timing
- Not using auto attack animations to pull camps (treating them as stationary fights)

### Quick Reference
- Always pull camps during auto attacks toward your next camp destination
- When your camps are up: only take ≥90% certainty plays; when down: use time for information/safe plays
- Employ click and pan between camps for information without sacrificing clear speed
- Base immediately after full clear to maintain tempo and camp timers
- When uncertain about enemy jungle presence: farm your own camps rather than risking invades

##Pathing & Routing

### Core Principles of Tempo-Based Pathing
- **Maximize clear speed**: Pull every camp toward your next destination using auto‑attacks (e.g., pull Gromp all the way to the next camp while auto‑attacking). This saves seconds per camp and prevents wasted walking time.
- **Kite toward the next camp**: Always move your champion in the direction of the following camp while clearing the current one; never stand still or walk back after a camp is dead.
- **Understand “camps up” vs. “camps down”**:
  - **Camps up**: You have just cleared a camp and it will respawn soon (typically ~2:00). You must only take **high‑percentage** plays (≥90% chance) – ganks, invades, or objectives – because leaving the jungle now puts you off‑tempo.
  - **Camps down**: The camp you just cleared is still down and you have free time before the next spawn. Lower‑percentage plays (e.g., scuttle attempts, deep wardings, smoke screens) are acceptable here.
- **Use spare time wisely**: After a full clear, you have a window (often 20‑30 seconds) before your next camp spawns. Spend it on:
  - Warding the enemy jungle to track pathing.
  - Sweeping enemy vision or placing deep wards.
  - Walking to a lane for a quick look or pick.
  - Recalling for items if you are low, then returning to the objective.
- **Base on tempo**: Return to base only when you are low on health/mana **and** you can still hit the next camp spawn on time (e.g., base after Krugs to be back for Gromp). Do not base for non‑essential reasons if it costs you a camp spawn.

### Jungle Tracking & Information Gathering
- **Download enemy jungler’s pathing**:
  - Place a ward in the enemy jungle (or use a control ward) as soon as you see them appear (e.g., when they show top after taking their red buff). This tells you their starting side, clear speed, and likely next camp.
  - If you cannot ward, observe when they appear on lane or at objectives; infer their clear from the camp they just took (e.g., seeing them at top lane after level 2 suggests they did red → wolves).
- **Mirror their position**: Assume the enemy jungler is on the opposite side of the map from you when you are at the clear. If you are on your second camp (blue), they are likely on their their second camp as well.
- **Track their camp timers relative to yours. If you are on your third camp and they are on theirs, they are pathing in sync with you; if they are pinging “up” (toward your jungle), they are likely invading or counter‑jungling.
- **Track summoner spells**: Use chat history or pings to note when the enemy laner/jungler used flash or other summoner spells. A missing flash dramatically increases gank success probability (e.g., “no flash = 90% chance gank”).
- **Predict enemy options after your play**: After you make a move (gank, invade, objective), immediately ask: “Where can the enemy jungler go now?” Ping those options for your team (e.g., if you took their red, they could be on your wolves, raptors, or looking for a scuttle).

### Route Planning: 3‑Camp vs. Full Clear
- **Three‑camp clear (early game)**:
  - Use when you need an early level 2 or 3 gank (e.g., red → wolves → blue) to apply lane pressure before the enemy jungler finishes their first clear.
  - Only viable on champions with strong early dueling or gank potential (e.g., Lee Sin, Elise). Weak clearers (e.g., Nasus, Shyvana) should avoid sacrificing full clear speed for a three‑camp rush unless the lane is highly volatile.
- **Full clear**:
  - Provides maximum gold, experience, and tempo advantage. Aim for a full clear when you want to hit level 3 with double buffs, secure the first scuttle, or simply out‑farm the enemy.
  - Adapt the order based on your champion’s clear speed and the enemy’s likely path: start on the side opposite the enemy jungler to avoid early contests, or start on the same side if you want to contest their first buff.
- **Decision factors**:
  - **Champion clear speed**: Slow clearers (Jax, Nasus) must micro‑optimize every auto‑attack and pull to stay on tempo; fast clearers (Shyvana, Graves) can afford slightly more flexible paths.
  - **Game state**: If your lane is losing and you need immediate impact, lean toward a three‑camp gank. If you are ahead or playing a scaling champion, prioritize full clear and tempo.
  - **Objective timers**: Align your clear to hit scuttle spawns (usually at 2:00) or dragon timers; a full clear that finishes just before scuttle lets you contest it without losing tempo.

### Adaptive Pathing & Decision Making
- **React to enemy jungler location**:
  - If you know the enemy is on the opposite side (e.g., you see them top while you are bot), you can safely take your own scuttle, invade their far jungle, or look for a counter‑gank.
  - If they are on your side, play defensively: ward deep, avoid over‑extending, and look for counter‑jungling opportunities when they leave.
- **Objective evaluation**:
  - **Scuttle crab**: Only contest if you have vision, the enemy is elsewhere, or a guaranteed kill on the lane nearby. If you are behind and the enemy has priority, let them take it and look for a gank elsewhere.
  - **Dragon**: Take dragon **only** if you are certain you can finish before the enemy can collapse. Check:
    - Enemy summoner spells (especially flash) on nearby laners.
    - Enemy jungler’s position and distance.
    - Your own smite availability and damage.
    - If unsure, do **not** take it; the risk of losing your jungle for a contested dragon is rarely worth it.
  - **Herald/Rift Herald**: Apply the same logic – only take if you can secure it with vision and a safe escape route.
- **Switching pathing after recalls or kills**:
  - After recalling, immediately check the minimap for enemy jungler position and lane states before deciding your next camp.
  - After a successful kill or assist, assess whether you have time to take a nearby camp, ward, or recall for items before the next camp spawns.
  - If you are low and far from your camps, base to reset, then path from the base toward the objective you need (e.g., base bot side to then walk top for top scuttle).
- **Use of “spare time” for macro plays**:
  - When your camps are down, you can safely:
    - Walk to mid lane to look for a pick or to relieve pressure.
    - Place deep wards in the enemy jungle to track their next move.
    - Attempt a low‑percentage gank if you have vision and the enemy is overextended.
    - Farm a small camp (e.g., wolves) to keep your gold income steady while waiting for a bigger play.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wasting time on low‑percentage plays**: Chasing a dead scuttle, diving a lane with flash up, or invading without vision when you are behind puts you into tempo debt.
- **Ignoring camps‑up vs. camps‑down**: Attempting ganks or invades when your camps are about to respawn (camps up) without a ≥90% chance to succeed.
- **Over‑committing to dragon without vision**: Taking dragon when the enemy can collapse and steal your jungle camps, often resulting in a net loss.
- **Failing to use spare time**: Sitting idle or walking aimlessly after a clear instead of warding, sweeping, or looking for picks.
- **Not tracking the enemy jungler**: Making decisions based on assumptions rather than actual ward information or observed positions, leading to bad invades or missed counter‑ganks.
- **Base for the wrong reasons**: Returning to base when you are not low or when it costs you a camp spawn, breaking your tempo.
- **Rigid route running**: Sticking to a pre‑planned path regardless of enemy movements, lane states, or objective timers.

### Quick Reference
- **Pull and kite every camp** – use auto‑attacks to pull camps toward your next destination to save seconds per clear.
- **Camps up = high‑% only; camps down = free time** – only take ≥90% chance ganks/objectives when your camps will respawn soon; otherwise you can safely scout, ward, or look for low‑percentage plays.
- **Ward enemy jungle to download pathing** – knowing where the enemy jungler is lets you prioritize ganks over scuttle, avoid invades, and time your own objective attempts.
- **Use spare time (20‑30 s after a clear) for vision, lane pressure, or a quick recall** – never sit idle; turn downtime into information or pressure.
- **Take dragon only when you are 100% sure you can finish before the enemy can respond** – if you have any doubt, skip it and look for a play elsewhere.

## Early Game (Levels 1-6)

### First Clear Decisions
- **Clear speed and camp order set your tempo.** A fast, structured clear lets you be back at your Grump when it spawns unless you have a guaranteed play.  
- **Standard vs. mirrored clears.** A full clear (e.g., Red → Raptors → Krugs → Gromp → Wolves → Blue) is safe; mirroring the enemy’s start (e.g., taking Raptors then Krugs if they began Raptors) often yields a smoother second clear.  
- **Jungle tracking at camp 3.** When you finish your third camp, glance at the minimap to see where the enemy jungler is on his third camp. If he’s also on his third camp, you know his pathing direction and can anticipate his next move (e.g., “Hecrim will go bot”).  
- **Invade timing based on enemy start.** If you see the enemy began Raptors, expect his Raptors to respawn ~2:00–2:15 and consider an invade after your clear if you have vision or a numbers advantage.  
- **Base timers and Smite efficiency.** Base when your blue is spawning and you have no other immediate objective. One‑shot camps with Smite (don’t hold it) – this saves roughly 10 seconds per clear and keeps your tempo high.  
- **When in doubt, farm.** If you lack a clear, high‑percentage play (invade, gank, or objective), default to farming your own camps rather than forcing a low‑percentage risk.

### Early Gank Windows
- **Level 2–3:** First realistic gank window after securing your first buff and hitting level 2. Look for lanes with hard CC or enemy summoner‑spell cooldowns.  
- **Level 3 power spike (double buffs).** After Red + Blue you have stronger dueling and 2v2/3v3 potential. Prioritize ganks where the enemy laner is missing Flash or is overextended.  
- **Level 4 power spike.** Usually reached after your first clear + Scuttle or after a second camp. Use the extra ability/stat boost to contest the first Scuttle, dive a lane with a summoner‑spell disadvantage, or look for a 2v2 skirmish.  
- **Use spare time for information.** After a clear, walk to ward enemy jungle entrances or check the minimap/chat for Flash, Heal, or Ult cooldowns before committing to a gank.  
- **High‑percentage gank rule.** Only gank when you have a numbers advantage, the enemy lacks a key summoner spell (especially Flash), or your lane provides hard CC that guarantees follow‑up.  
- **If no guaranteed play exists,** revert to farming or contesting the nearest Scuttle rather than forcing a risky dive.

### Scuttle Crab
- **First Scuttle (~2:00).** Contest only if you have vision or a numbers advantage; otherwise, give it up and use the time to farm or invade the enemy’s weak side.  
- **Second Scuttle (~3:15).** This spawn is a major tempo swing. If you have a level or numbers lead (e.g., you’re level 4 vs. enemy level 3), you can often win it outright.  
- **Vision on Dragon pit.** When fighting for Scuttle, keep vision on the Dragon pit. Giving up Dragon for a Scuttle is rarely worth it unless you have a clear follow‑up plan (e.g., a guaranteed gank).  
- **If you lose the Scuttle,** do not over‑extend for Dragon; instead, reset to your own camps or look for a counter‑gank on the opposite side.  
- **Use spare time after a clear** to walk to the nearest Scuttle and secure it with Smite; this often yields a better return than wandering aimlessly.

### Level 3/4 Power Spikes
- **Level 3 (double buffs).** Provides increased damage and durability. Look for 2v2/3v3 skirmishes where you can flank or for lanes where the enemy has no Flash.  
- **Level 4 (after first clear + Scuttle or second camp).** Unlocks your second basic ability or gives a stronger all‑in. Use it to contest the first Dragon, secure a Scuttle, or dive a lane with a summoner‑spell disadvantage.  
- **Track enemy level via camp timers.** If you see the enemy still on his second camp while you are on your third, you have a tempo advantage and can play more aggressively.  
- **Weak early‑game champs (e.g., Shyvana, Nasus).** Prioritize farming and jungle tracking over early ganks; wait for your level 6 power spike.  
- **Level 6 spike.** When your ultimate is up and the enemy laner has no Flash, you can often guarantee a kill. Communicate this to your lane (“enemy bot no flash, I have ult at 6”) and collapse for the kill.

### Common Mistakes
- **Neglecting jungle tracker:** walking into enemy invades or missing gank chances because you didn’t note the enemy’s camp timer.  
- **Holding Smite unnecessarily:** losing ~10 seconds each clear by not one‑shotting camps.  
- **Forcing ganks/Scuttles without vision, numbers, or summoner‑spell advantage:** leading to avoidable deaths.  
- **Over‑extending for Dragon after losing a Scuttle:** allowing the enemy jungler to vacuum your side of the jungle.  
- **Poor mental stack:** chatting, being on Discord, or not clearing distractions, which reduces information processing and leads to bad decisions.  
- **Wasting spare time:** idling or wandering after a clear instead of using it to ward, check summoner spells, or farm information.  
- **Basing at the wrong time:** recalling too early (missing farm) or too late (losing tempo); base only when you have nothing else to do (e.g., blue spawning) and can do so safely.  
- **Taking Dragon without vision or follow‑up:** giving the enemy jungler free rein to steal your camps while you’re committed elsewhere.

### Quick Reference
- **Clear fast, be back at Grump on spawn unless you have a guaranteed play.**  
- **Track enemy jungle:** after your third camp, note his third‑camp position to predict his path.  
- **One‑shot camps with Smite (~10 s saved per clear); base when blue is spawning and you have nothing else to do.**  
- **Contest Scuttle only with vision or numbers; the second Scuttle (~3:15) is a major tempo swing.**  
- **Look for level‑3 (double buffs) and level‑4 spikes to gank when enemy lacks Flash or is overextended.**  
- **When in doubt, farm your own camps rather than risking low‑percentage plays.**  
- **Communicate flash‑advantage opportunities (e.g., “enemy bot no flash, I have ult at 6”) and collapse with your lane.**

  - Current death timers (early game = short timers, late game = longer timers).  
  - Whether you have Smite ready (Smite gives you a secure finish; without it, the window shrinks).  
  - Enemy summoner spells (e.g., no Flash = harder for them to contest or follow up).  
  - Enemy positioning (if their jungler is on the opposite side, they likely can’t collapse quickly).  
- **If unsure, don’t take** – When you cannot confidently answer “yes” to the above, abandon the Dragon attempt. Preserving your side camps and lane pressure is often worth more than a contested Dragon.  
- **Trade side camps for Dragon only when it’s favorable** – Taking Dragon should not cost you your entire jungle side. If the enemy jungler (e.g., Hecarim, Lee Sin) can vacuum your camps while you’re on Dragon, it’s usually a net loss unless you have a clear follow‑up kill or vision advantage.  
- **Leverage flash/no‑flash information** – Knowing an enemy laner (or jungler) lacks Flash creates a kill window. After securing that kill, you can often take Dragon safely before they can respond.  
- **Win the smite fight** – If you have Smite and the enemy does not (or theirs is on cooldown), you can contest Dragon even with equal numbers by smiting it out from under them.  
- **Focus on what’s next** – After taking Dragon (or deciding not to), immediately shift your mindset to the next play: lane pressure, invading the enemy jungle, setting up for the next objective, or resetting to farm. Do not over‑commit to the Dragon pit itself.  
- **Let the enemy waste resources** – If the enemy commits multiple ultimates, teleports, or summoner spells to contest Dragon, you can sometimes disengage, let them waste those cooldowns, then look to steal a buff or catch them out of position.  
- **Later Dragons give more leeway** – As the game progresses, death timers lengthen, making it safer to take Dragon even if the enemy is nearby, because they need more time to regroup. Early Dragons (≈9 min) require a tighter window and stronger vision advantage.

### Baron & Rift Herald (Application of the Same Principles)
- The coaches did not discuss Baron or Rift Herald explicitly, but the decision‑making framework for Dragon transfers directly:  
  1. **Vision** on the pit is mandatory.  
  2. **Calculate enemy response time** (death timers, travel distance, summoner spells).  
  3. **Compare Smite availability** and numbers advantage.  
  4. **Weigh the trade** – taking Baron/Rift Herald should not gift the enemy a free jungle clear or turret plates.  
  5. **Focus on the next play** after securing or denying the objective.  
- Apply the same “can they make it back?” question, using the longer respawn timers of Baron (later game) to your advantage.

### Objective Priority & Setup
- **Priority is situational** – There is no universal “Dragon > Baron” rule; priority depends on:  
  - Your team’s win condition (early game snowball vs. late‑game scaling).  
  - Enemy composition (e.g., heavy engage makes early Dragon riskier).  
  - Current gold/experience leads and map state (tower plates, inhibitor timers).  
- **Setup before the objective** –  
  - Place deep wards in the enemy jungle and river 30‑45 seconds before the objective spawns.  
  - Ping your team to collapse or retreat based on enemy vision.  
  - If you have a lead, consider setting up a slow push in a side lane to force the enemy to choose between defending the objective or losing lane pressure.  
  - Use your spare time after a clear to position yourself for a smite contest or to bait the enemy into over‑committing.

### Trading Objectives for Side Pressure
- **Do not over‑trade** – Taking Dragon/Baron/Rift Herald is only worth it if you retain at least equal map pressure afterward. If the enemy can take two of your camps or a turret while you’re on the pit, the trade is usually negative unless you secure a kill or force a summoner spell.  
- **When behind, prioritize side camps and vision** – If you are behind, giving up an objective to avoid losing your entire jungle is often the correct play; rebuild vision and farm before retrying.  
- **When ahead, you can afford to contest** – A lead gives you the buffer to lose some camps while still coming out ahead if you secure the objective and win the ensuing fight.  
- **Use the objective as a bait** – If you suspect the enemy will over‑commit to Dragon, you can fake a contest, then collapse on them with your own jungler and laners for a pick.

## Common Mistakes
- Taking Dragon without any vision on the pit or river.  
- Ignoring death timers and attempting Dragon when the enemy can arrive before you finish.  
- Over‑committing to Dragon and losing your entire jungle side for little gain.  
- Forgetting to use Smite (or wasting it on a camp) when contesting an objective.  
- Focusing solely on securing the objective and not thinking about the follow‑up play.  
- Not tracking the enemy jungler’s position after a clear, leading to surprise collapses.  
- Disregarding enemy summoner spells (e.g., Flash) when assessing kill windows.  
- Giving up an objective too easily when you actually have a winning fight or vision advantage.  
- Failing to reset vision after an unsuccessful contest, leaving you blind for the next spawn.  
- Using spare time inefficiently (e.g., wandering instead of warding or tracking).

## Quick Reference
- **Vision first** – Ward Dragon/Baron/Rift Herald pits before you commit.  
- **Ask “Can they make it back?”** – Check death timers, Smite, enemy summoner spells, and jungler location.  
- **If unsure, don’t take** – Preserve your side camps and lane pressure over a contested objective.

##Mid Game Macro (Levels 6-14)

### Tempo Management
- **Maintain tempo advantage**: After securing kills or objectives, immediately base and move to the next objective (e.g., after bot lane double kill, insta base and go for top crab) to match or exceed enemy jungler's tempo
- **Base timing efficiency**: Base when it's efficient (when you have nothing else to do); even waiting 10 seconds for Krugs spawn is still a valid base timer
- **Clear speed optimization**: Every auto attack should pull camps toward your next destination; walking between camps without pulling wastes significant time (20-30 seconds per clear)
- **Tempo debt awareness**: Falling behind in tempo creates cumulative disadvantage; if enemy gets double crab while you're still clearing, you're now behind on both XP and map pressure
- **Spare time utilization**: When camps are down (after clear), use spare time to sweep vision, prepare for next camps, or look for low-percentage plays rather than forcing high-risk actions

### Information Gathering Framework
- **Minimap prompts as primary source**: 70% of information comes from minimap - watch for icons flashing, health bars dropping, and ward placements as triggers to investigate lanes
- **Click and pan technique**: When moving between camps, click your destination and pan camera to lanes in your proximity (e.g., when running from Krugs to Raptors, check bot lane if you're blue side)
- **Summoner spell tracking**: Prioritize tracking enemy flash status - having no flash is like putting a crosshair on their head and significantly increases gank success probability
- **Chat monitoring**: Only summoner spell information in chat is relevant; ignore everything else (toxicity, shotcalling attempts)
- **Enemy jungler pathing download**: Ward enemy jungle camps to know their exact pathing and timers; this allows you to predict their movements and counterplay effectively
- **Proximity-based awareness**: When clearing camps, always check lanes closest to your current position first for actionable information

### Decision Making: Match/Trade/Minimize Framework
- **Match when stronger**: If you have numbers advantage (more ults, better items, ahead in levels), take fights you believe you can win (e.g., 3v3 bot when your lane has priority)
- **Trade when weaker**: If enemy is taking dragon and your bot lane is dead or weak, give up dragon to take enemy jungle camps or pressure elsewhere rather than contesting losing fights
- **Minimize when equal/same side**: When on same side of map as enemy or when chances are equal, "just give the objective" (e.g., let them take dragon) and use the time to take your camps or prepare for next wave
- **Process of elimination**: Use enemy jungler's known position to infer their absence elsewhere (if they're top side, they're likely not bot side and vice versa)
- **Gank probability threshold**: Only attempt ganks when you're 90% certain of success based on enemy summoner spells, positioning, and your own cooldowns

### Camps Up vs Camps Down Concept
- **Camps UP (farming time)**: Only take actions you believe are 90%+ likely to succeed (high-percentage ganks, invades, objectives); avoid random aggression or flash burns
- **Camps DOWN (spare time)**: Free time to look for anything - sweep vision, check for invades, prepare for next camps, or take lower-percentage plays since you're not behind on farm
- **Transition recognition**: Know exactly when your last camp finishes (Gromp, Krugs, etc.) and immediately assess map state before deciding next move
- **Base timing alignment**: Your base timer should align with when your camps will be back up; don't linger in base unnecessarily

### Jungle Tracking and Pathing
- **Path prediction**: When you see enemy jungler at a camp, predict their next 2-3 camps based on standard pathing (e.g., if they're at your wolves, they're likely going to your blue next)
- **Tempo comparison**: Constantly compare your jungle tempo to enemy's - are you ahead, even, or behind? This dictates your aggression level
- **Counter-pathing**: If enemy jungler is pathing top to bot, you can safely take their top side camps when they're bot side (and vice versa)
- **Vision denial**: Use knowledge of enemy pathing to place deep wards in their jungle to maintain information advantage
- **Invade timing**: Only invade enemy jungle when you know they're on the opposite side of the map and have no immediate threat to your laners

### Power Spikes and Level 6 Play
- **Level 6 power spike**: At level 6 with ultimate, look for guaranteed kill opportunities (e.g., enemy laner with no flash); this is often your strongest window for impact
- **Ultimate timing**: Don't waste ultimate on low-percentage plays; save it for when you have near-certain kill potential (e.g., enemy with no flash + hard CC from laner)
- **Post-level 6 pathing**: After hitting 6, prioritize getting your ultimate online quickly through efficient clearing rather than forcing early ganks
- **Spike recognition**: Identify your champion's specific power spikes (item completion, level breakpoints) and align your aggression with those windows

### Objective Control
- **Dragon decision making**: Only take dragon if you can win the fight or if enemy cannot contest in time (check death timers, enemy positioning, and your own readiness)
- **Scuttle crab priority**: Prioritize scuttle crabs over random ganks when you have vision advantage or know enemy jungler is elsewhere
- **Objective trading**: If you can't win an objective fight, immediately look for equivalent value elsewhere (e.g., if they take dragon, take their jungle camps or pressure a lane)
- **Vision control around objectives**: Sweep and ward objective pits 20-30 seconds before spawn to deny enemy information and enable safe contesting
- **Objective timing awareness**: Track objective spawn timers (dragon: 5 min, herald: 6:30, baron: 20 min) and be in position 10-15 seconds before spawn

### Split Decision Making (Group vs Split)
- **Group when**: You have numbers advantage, enemy is grouped and vulnerable, or you need to secure vision around an objective
- **Split when**: You have strong side lane matchups, enemy is weak on one side, or you can create pressure on multiple maps simultaneously
- **Split safety**: When split pushing, ensure you have escape routes and vision; never split without knowing enemy jungler's position
- **Transition recognition**: Know when to collapse from split to group (e.g., when dragon spawns, when enemy groups for turret dive)
- **Pressure conversion**: Convert split pressure into objective control (e.g., split bot lane pressure to enable dragon take)

### Playing From Behind/Ahead
- **When ahead (7-8/10 works)**: Match enemy aggression, take fights when you have advantage, maintain vision control, and look to extend lead
- **When behind (2-10 works)**: Minimize risks, wait for enemy mistakes (e.g., no flash), avoid forcing plays, and focus on farming and vision rather than aggression
- **Mental reset after death**: When you die, immediately think "what's next?" rather than dwelling on mistakes; this maintains decision-making quality
- **Adaptive pathing**: If behind, adjust your path to avoid enemy jungler and look for safe farm; if ahead, look to invade and deny enemy jungle
- **Objective concession**: When behind, it's often correct to give up objectives to avoid worse losses (e.g., "just give the drake" and take your camps)

### Base Timing and Efficiency
- **Efficient base triggers**: Base when you have nothing else to do (camps down, no immediate threats, inventory full enough to recall)
- **Base duration value**: 8 seconds of basing is worth it with ~1000+ gold; less gold means you should stay out longer to farm
- **Itemization timing**: Think about your build during champ select/loading screen to free up mental space during game; don't theorycraft in base
- **Recall readiness**: Always be ready to recall immediately after clearing your last camp if no guaranteed plays exist
- **Base location efficiency**: Base closest to your next objective to minimize travel time (e.g., base near top lane if going for top crab)

### Common Mistakes
- Wasting time looking for dead camps when behind in tempo instead of seeking guaranteed plays elsewhere
- Forcing ganks or fights without 90%+ success probability based on enemy summoner spells and positioning
- Ignoring minimap prompts and summoner spell information in chat when making decisions
- Not adjusting aggression level based on camps up/down state (forcing plays when camps are up)
- Failing to track enemy jungler pathing and tempo, leading to poor invade/counter-jungle decisions
- Overstaying in base or inefficiently using spare time after clears
- Not recognizing power spikes and misaligning aggression with champion strength windows
- Forcing objective fights when behind or without numbers advantage
- Splitting without proper vision or knowledge of enemy jungler position
- Reviewing mistakes during gameplay instead of staying locked in and thinking "what's next?"
- Not using click and pan technique when moving between camps to gather lane information
- Holding smite unnecessarily instead of one-shotting camps for optimal clear speed
- Getting distracted by external factors (Discord, shotcalling attempts) instead of maintaining information processing focus

### Quick Reference
- **Camps Up/Down Rule**: Only take 90%+ probability plays when camps are up; use spare time (camps down) for vision, preparation, or lower-percentage plays
- **Match/Trade/Minimize**: Match when stronger (numbers advantage), trade when weaker, minimize when equal/same side ("just give the objective")
- **Level 6 Power Spike**: At level 6, prioritize guaranteed kill opportunities (enemy no flash + laner CC) over farming or random aggression
- **Information Hierarchy**: 70% minimap prompts → Summoner spell tracking (especially flash) → Enemy jungler pathing via wards → Proximity-based lane checks when moving between camps
- **Tempo Maintenance**: After kills/objectives, insta base and move to next objective to maintain or gain tempo advantage over enemy jungler

## Late Game & Teamfighting

### Late Game Jungle Role
- **Maintain high clear speed** – prioritize fast camp clears to keep tempo; only use spare time for purposeful actions, not random ganks.  
- **Use spare time intentionally** – while moving between camps, click‑and‑pan your camera to lanes to gather information (icons flashing, health bars dropping, enemy summoner spell usage).  
- **Track the enemy jungler** – note when they are on their third camp, watch for pings up/down, and predict their path to bot/top/mid, dragon/baron, or your/ their buffs.  
- **Vision control** – place control wards and sweep enemy wards during spare time; having vision on dragon/baron is a key low‑elo vs high‑elo differentiator.  
- **After a lost fight, reset instantly** – base immediately to avoid further losses and ping the enemy summoner spells used (flash, barrier, ghost, etc.) to track cooldowns.  
- **Focus on “what’s next”** – rather than dwelling on a mistake, quickly reassess the map for the next play (pick, objective, or counter‑jungle).  
- **Avoid over‑committing to objectives** – if taking dragon/baron would leave your jungle open to the enemy jungler vacuuming your camps, only contest when you have vision or the enemy jungler is elsewhere.  

### Teamfight Positioning
- **Position to maximize your ult’s impact** – look for enemies without flash; if you have your ultimate ready, you can secure a kill by ulting them toward your team.  
- **Use flash decisively** – save flash for game‑changing engages or to turn a losing 2v2/3v3 into a win; do not waste it on low‑value trades.  
- **Stay on the fringe of fights** – if your champion is weak early (e.g., Shyvana, Nasus), avoid diving the enemy backline; instead, wait for engages from your team and follow up with peel or clean‑up.  
- **Keep an eye on the mini‑map during fights** – continue to gather information (enemy summoner spell cooldowns, enemy jungler location) even while fighting.  
- **Do not give up vision for a fight** – if you must leave vision on an objective to join a fight, ensure the trade is worth it (e.g., you have a guaranteed kill or objective).  

### Engage/Peel
- **Engage when the enemy lacks flash** – if you have ult or a strong engage tool and the target has no flash, commit (e.g., ult → follow‑up).  
- **Peel for carries** – when you are the frontline, use your abilities to block enemy divers and protect your backline; prioritize keeping your ADC/mid alive over chasing kills.  
- **Use smite to secure objectives during fights** – if you are contesting dragon/baron in a teamfight, smite the objective as soon as it is low to deny the enemy steal.  
- **Counter‑engage with flash** – if the enemy engages on your carry, flash to disrupt or reposition and protect them.  
- **Avoid over‑extending** – if you are behind or your champion is weak, do not chase deep into the enemy backline; instead, stay near your team and look for picks on the outskirts.  

### Baron/Elder Dances
- **Maintain vision on Baron/Elder** – place deep wards and sweep enemy vision; this prevents the enemy jungler from taking your camps while you contest.  
- **Track enemy jungler path to Baron** – use spare time to watch for the enemy jungler moving toward the river/baron; if they are heading there, either contest with vision or look for a pick elsewhere.  
- **Do not trade your jungle for Baron** – if taking Baron would leave your entire side exposed and the enemy jungler is strong at invading (e.g., Hecarim, Lee Sin), only contest when you have vision advantage or the enemy jungler is elsewhere.  
- **Use spare time to look for second scuttle/river vision** – even in the late game, a quick sweep or ward in the river can reveal enemy rotations and prevent surprise Baron steals.  
- **After a Baron fight, reset and reassess** – if you lose the fight, base immediately, ping enemy summoner spells, and look for the next play (e.g., enemy over‑extending, a side lane push, or a pick on the enemy jungler).  

### Common Mistakes
- **Wasting spare time on random ganks** instead of using it for vision, tracking, or objective preparation.  
- **Face‑checking without vision** when moving between camps or entering the river, leading to unnecessary deaths.  
- **Over‑committing to dragon/baron** without checking enemy jungler location, allowing them to steal your jungle camps.  
- **Failing to track enemy summoner spell cooldowns** after a fight, missing engage windows.  
- **Using flash on low‑value trades** instead of saving it for game‑changing engages or peel.  
- **Staying too far forward in teamfights** with weak early‑game champions, getting caught out and dying.  
- **Neglecting to ping enemy summoner spells** after a skirmish, losing track of key cooldowns (flash, ult, etc.).  
- **Ignoring the “what’s next” mindset** and dwelling on mistakes, causing delayed reactions to emerging opportunities.  

### Quick Reference
- **Keep tempo:** clear camps fast; use spare time for vision, tracking, or purposeful ganks.  
- **Vision is power:** ward and sweep dragon/baron; vision prevents enemy jungle steals.  
- **Engage smart:** ult or flash onto enemies without flash; peel for carries when you’re weak.  
- **Reset after losses:** base instantly, ping enemy summoner spells, then look for the next play.  
- **Track the enemy jungler:** note third camp, pings, and path to lanes/objectives/buffs during spare time.

## Mental Framework & Decision-Making

### Core Philosophy & Mindset

- **Treat games like exams**: View each game as a test with 50 "questions" (decisions) you can answer correctly or incorrectly regardless of win/loss outcome. Your rank is determined by your average correct answers over a large sample size (100+ games), not individual results.
  
- **Focus on decision quality, not outcomes**: Stop judging your performance solely by wins/losses. Instead, evaluate whether you made the best possible decisions given the information available at each moment.

- **Be curiosity-driven, not pain-driven**: When reviewing losses, approach them with curiosity to learn ("What could I have done better?") rather than frustration ("Why is this happening to me?"). This transforms losses into valuable data.

- **Recognize contextual wins/losses**: Understand that some wins are "free" (carried by strong teammates) and some losses are "unlucky" (despite good decisions). Neither should significantly impact your long-term development.

- **Control what you can**: Focus exclusively on your own decisions and actions. Teammate performance, enemy luck, and game outcomes are outside your direct control and should not affect your mental state.

- **Embrace the 70/15/15 rule**: Expect approximately 70% of games to be "control games" where your decisions determine the outcome, 15% to be unwinnable regardless of your play, and 15% to be free wins. Your climb comes from consistently performing well in the 70% control games.

### Decision Frameworks

- **Match/Trade/Minimize principle**:
  - **MATCH** when you have clear advantage (numbers, ultimates, items, vision): Take fights, objectives, and aggressive plays
  - **TRADE** when you're weaker or equal: Take enemy resources (camps, vision) instead of forcing unfavorable fights
  - **MINIMIZE** when contesting would cost more than yielding: Give up objectives (dragon, baron) if fighting would risk greater losses

- **Camps Up vs. Camps Down decision framework**:
  - **CAMPS UP** (your jungle camps are alive): Only take high-percentage (≥90%) ganks, invades, or objective plays
  - **CAMPS DOWN** (your jungle camps are down): Use this "free time" for lower-percentage plays, information gathering, vision control, or lane presence

- **Process of elimination for enemy jungler tracking**:
   - If you took top scuttle, enemy jungler likely took bot scuttle (and vice versa)
   - If you know enemy jungler started on one side, track their pathing to predict their next location
   - Use ward information to confirm or eliminate potential enemy jungler paths

- **Spare time utilization**: After clearing a camp, use the time until your next camp spawns for:
   - Information gathering (minimap checks, camera panning)
   - Vision placement/denial
   - Lane presence or threat projection
   - Preparing for your next objective or play

### Information Gathering & Processing

- **Minimap awareness as primary information source** (~70% of relevant data):
   - Watch for health bars dropping and ability icons flashing (summoner spell usage)
   - Notice when multiple enemies converge on a lane (potential fight)
   - Track enemy jungler relative to your position through camp timers

- **Intentional camera panning (Click and Pan technique)**:
   - Click on minimap to jump to area of interest
   - Pan camera deliberately to lanes in your proximity
   - Have a specific question before panning (e.g., "Is mid lane pushed?" or "Did bot lane use flash?")
   - Use time between camps to gather information while moving

- **Chat and summoner spell monitoring**:
   - Treat chat as a summoner spell tracker (ignore non-essential talk)
   - Critical information: enemy summoner spell cooldowns (especially Flash)
   - When allies ping "no flash" or similar, treat this as high-value tactical data
   - Mute distracting teammates but keep party chat active for essential pings

- **Mental stack management**:
   - Clear your mind before games by planning your build/pathing in champion select
   - Avoid distractions (Discord, screen sharing, secondary monitors) during gameplay
   - Treat your brain like a CPU: close unnecessary background processes to free up decision-making power
   - Build habits to automate routine actions (camp paths, ward timings) so they require minimal conscious thought

### Tempo Management

- **Optimize clear speed**:
   - Prioritize fast, efficient jungle clears as a foundation for tempo advantage
   - Don't sacrifice clear speed for uncertain invades or early ganks
   - Track your clear timing relative to enemy jungler through vision and camp control

- **Base timing efficiency**:
   - Only base when the gold value exceeds the time spent (e.g., 950+ gold for 8 seconds of basing)
   - Base when you have nothing else to do (camps down, no immediate play opportunities)
   - One-shot camps with smite rather than holding it for potential steals (wastes tempo)

- **Structured tempo maintenance**:
   - Always be back at your grump (or next camp) when it spawns unless a guaranteed play exists
   - Use spare time effectively: walk toward lanes while gaining information instead of standing still
   - Match enemy jungler's tempo: respond to their aggressive plays with your own counter-plays

- **Camp mirroring and positioning**:
   - Mirror enemy jungler's position relative to your own when possible
   - Use vision to confirm enemy jungler location before making decisions
   - Adjust your pathing based on enemy jungler's predicted location to avoid or create advantageous encounters

### Adapting to Game State

- **Playing from behind adjustments**:
   - Accept you have fewer opportunities (perhaps only 2/10 plays work vs. 7-8/10 when ahead)
   - Minimize risks: avoid forcing plays, wait for enemy mistakes
   - Trade when possible (take enemy resources), minimize when contesting is unfavorable
   - Focus exclusively on "what's next" after deaths or setbacks, not on what went wrong

- **Objective evaluation framework**:
   - Before contesting an objective, ask: "Can the enemy make it back before I finish?"
   - Consider death timers, travel distance, and vision control
   - Only take objectives when you can safely contest them; otherwise, yield and look for alternatives

- **Lane state assessment**:
   - Evaluate lane priority based on: wave state, summoner spell availability, enemy positioning
   - Don't gank pushed lanes unless you have overwhelming advantage (e.g., enemy has no Flash)
   - Use process of elimination: if you took top scuttle, enemy jungler is likely bot-side

- **Champion and build adaptation**:
   - Think about your item build before the game (champion select) to free up mental space
   - Adapt your pathing and playstyle based on your champion's power spikes and weaknesses
   - Recognize when your champion is weak early (e.g., Shyvana, Nasus) and prioritize farming over fighting

### Tilt Management & Emotional Control

- **The "what's next" mindset**:
   - Immediately after dying or a bad play, ask: "What is the next productive action I can take?"
   - Never dwell on mistakes during gameplay; save analysis for post-game review
   - Treat each moment as a new decision point unburdened by previous outcomes

- **Emotional neutrality in decision-making**:
   - Make decisions based on available information, not frustration or excitement
   - Recognize when emotions are degrading your decision quality (e.g., forcing plays after a loss)
   - Reset your emotional state after each death or significant event

- **Avoiding result-oriented thinking**:
   - Separate your self-worth from game outcomes
   - Understand that even perfect decision-making can result in losses due to variance
   - Focus on long-term trends (win rate over 100+ games) rather than short-term streaks

- **Constructive post-game review**:
   - Review games with curiosity: "What decisions could I have improved?"
   - Identify 1-2 specific actions to improve per game, regardless of outcome
   - Celebrate good decisions even in losses; learn from bad decisions even in wins

### Common Mistakes

- **Indecision**: Hesitating between options wastes more time than making a suboptimal but decisive choice
- **Blindly following ally pings**: Acting on incomplete information (e.g., top laner pinging dive without knowing enemy jungler location)
- **Ignoring summoner spell information**: Failing to track enemy Flash, Heal, or other critical cooldowns from chat
- **Forcing low-percentage plays**: Taking fights or objectives when camps are up but success chance is <90%
- **Wasting spare time**: Standing still or farming inefficiently during camp respawn timers
- **Overvaluing objectives**: Risking significant losses to contest dragon/baron when minimizing would be better
- **Neglecting mental stack management**: Allowing distractions (Discord, multitasking) to impair information processing
- **Failing to adapt to game state**: Using the same aggressive playstyle when behind as when ahead
- **Result fixation**: Letting win/loss streaks affect confidence and decision quality in subsequent games
- **Not using process of elimination**: Failing to deduce enemy jungler location from your own actions and vision

### Quick Reference

- **Decision mantra**: "Match when stronger, trade when weaker, minimize when on same side"
- **Tempo rule**: Camps up = only ≥90% plays; Camps down = free time for information/gathering
- **Information priority**: Minimap (70%) > Chat/summoner spells > Camera panning with intention
- **Post-death action**: Immediately ask "What's next?" instead of dwelling on mistakes
- **Base efficiency rule**: Only base when gold gained > (time spent × 100 gold/minute) (e.g., 800+ gold for 8 seconds)

## Champion Matchups & Picks

### Jungle Champion Pool & Early Game Strengths
- **Weak early‑game junglers** (e.g., **Shyvana**, **Nasus**) lack strong dueling power before level 6. Prioritize farming, vision, and tracking over early fights.  
- **Strong early‑game junglers** (e.g., **Hecarim**) can apply pressure, invade, and contest scuttles early. Use this strength to gain tempo and deny the enemy jungle.  
- **Flash‑dependent champions** (e.g., **Shyvana**, **Jarvan IV**) become far more threatening when the enemy lacks flash. Track enemy summoner spells to know when you have a flash advantage.  

### Matchup Knowledge: Tracking & Pathing
- **Third‑camp tracking:** When you finish your third camp, note where the enemy jungler is.  
  - If they are also on their third camp, they are likely mirroring your path.  
  - If they are pinging/appearing on the opposite side, they are likely invading or heading to the other lane.  
- **Camp mirroring:** Position yourself opposite the enemy jungler’s location to predict their next move (e.g., if they are on your topside, expect them to clear their bottom side).  
- **Spare‑time usage:** After a full clear, use the ~20‑30 second window before your next camp respawns to:  
  - Sweep wards or place deep vision in the enemy jungle.  
  - Look for lane opportunities (e.g., check if a mid laner is missing an escape tool like Fizz E).  
  - Reset (base) if you are low or have lost a fight.  
- **Dragon vision control:** Before attempting dragon, ensure you have vision on the pit. Walking into dragon without vision gives the enemy jungler a “green light” to take all your camps while you are occupied.  

### Counter‑Picking & Playstyle Adaptation
- **Pick according to your game plan:**  
  - Choose a scaling jungler (Shyvana, Nasus) when your team can survive early and you intend to farm to a power spike.  
  - Choose an early‑pressure jungler (Hecarim) when you need to win early skirmishes, secure scuttles, or snowball lanes.  
- **Adjust to enemy jungler strength:**  
  - If the enemy jungler is strong early, play safer, focus on vision, and look for counter‑ganks when they over‑extend.  
  - If the enemy jungler is weak early, invade their jungle, take their camps, and deny them experience.  
- **Use flash advantage:** Before engaging, check if the enemy’s flash is on cooldown (via pinging or observing recent fights). If they lack flash, you can all‑in with your ultimate or combo (e.g., Shyvana ult + smite).  
- **Smite timing:** Save smite for objective contests (dragon, baron, scuttle) and to secure buffs when you suspect the enemy jungler is nearby.  

### Team Comp Awareness & Objective Control
- **Lane priority matters:** If your bot lane has priority (ward advantage, turret advantage, or summoner spells up), you can safely look for bot plays; otherwise, concede pressure and look for topside opportunities (e.g., top scuttle).  
- **Communication:** Ping enemy summoner spell usage (flash, barrier, ghost) and objective timers so your team knows when to follow up or retreat.  
- **Adapt to game tempo:**  
  - In slow games, use extra spare time for deep vision, counter‑jungling, and farming.  
  - In fast games, focus on high‑impact actions: securing scuttles, contesting dragon with smite, and looking for flash‑advantage picks.  
- **Objective trade‑offs:** Do not trade a large portion of your jungle (e.g., entire blue side) for a single objective unless you have vision and a guaranteed win.  

### Common Mistakes
- Taking dragon without vision, allowing the enemy jungler to vacuum your camps.  
- Ignoring enemy jungler’s third‑camp pathing and getting caught in counter‑jungles.  
- Wasting spare time after a clear instead of using it for vision, tracking, or resetting.  
- Fighting early with weak‑early junglers (Shyvana/Nasus) without a flash or level advantage.  
- Failing to track enemy summoner spells (especially flash) before committing to an all‑in.  
- Giving up all pressure after a lost fight instead of instantly basing and looking for the next play.  
- Mirroring incorrectly, ending up out of position and vulnerable to invades.  

### Quick Reference
- **Track the enemy jungler’s third camp** to predict their pathing.  
- **Use spare time after each clear** for vision, ward placement, or resetting.  
- **Only engage when you have a flash or ultimate advantage** (enemy flash down).  
- **Maintain dragon vision** before attempting the objective.  
- **Match your pick to your game plan:** scaling junglers for farm‑heavy compos, early‑pressure junglers for aggressive, tempo‑driven teams.
