##Pathing & Routing

### Core Principles of Tempo-Based Pathing
- **Maximize clear speed**: Pull every camp toward your next destination using auto‑attacks (e.g., pull Gromp all the way to the next camp while auto‑attacking). This saves seconds per camp and prevents wasted walking time.
- **Kite toward the next camp**: Always move your champion in the direction of the following camp while clearing the current one; never stand still or walk back after a camp is dead.
- **Understand “camps up” vs. “camps down”**:
  - **Camps up**: You have just cleared a camp and it will respawn soon (typically ~2:00). You must only take **high‑percentage** plays (≥90% chance) – ganks, invades, or objectives – because leaving the jungle now puts you off‑tempo.
  - **Camps down**: The camp you just cleared is still down and you have free time before the next spawn. Lower‑percentage plays (e.g., scuttle attempts, deep wardings, smoke screens) are acceptable here.
- **Use spare time wisely**: After a full clear, you have a window (often 20‑30 seconds) before your next camp spawns. Spend it on:
  - Warding the enemy jungle to track pathing.
  - Sweeping enemy vision or placing deep wards.
  - Walking to a lane for a quick look or pick.
  - Recalling for items if you are low, then returning to the objective.
- **Base on tempo**: Return to base only when you are low on health/mana **and** you can still hit the next camp spawn on time (e.g., base after Krugs to be back for Gromp). Do not base for non‑essential reasons if it costs you a camp spawn.

### Jungle Tracking & Information Gathering
- **Download enemy jungler’s pathing**:
  - Place a ward in the enemy jungle (or use a control ward) as soon as you see them appear (e.g., when they show top after taking their red buff). This tells you their starting side, clear speed, and likely next camp.
  - If you cannot ward, observe when they appear on lane or at objectives; infer their clear from the camp they just took (e.g., seeing them at top lane after level 2 suggests they did red → wolves).
- **Mirror their position**: Assume the enemy jungler is on the opposite side of the map from you when you are at the clear. If you are on your second camp (blue), they are likely on their their second camp as well.
- **Track their camp timers relative to yours. If you are on your third camp and they are on theirs, they are pathing in sync with you; if they are pinging “up” (toward your jungle), they are likely invading or counter‑jungling.
- **Track summoner spells**: Use chat history or pings to note when the enemy laner/jungler used flash or other summoner spells. A missing flash dramatically increases gank success probability (e.g., “no flash = 90% chance gank”).
- **Predict enemy options after your play**: After you make a move (gank, invade, objective), immediately ask: “Where can the enemy jungler go now?” Ping those options for your team (e.g., if you took their red, they could be on your wolves, raptors, or looking for a scuttle).

### Route Planning: 3‑Camp vs. Full Clear
- **Three‑camp clear (early game)**:
  - Use when you need an early level 2 or 3 gank (e.g., red → wolves → blue) to apply lane pressure before the enemy jungler finishes their first clear.
  - Only viable on champions with strong early dueling or gank potential (e.g., Lee Sin, Elise). Weak clearers (e.g., Nasus, Shyvana) should avoid sacrificing full clear speed for a three‑camp rush unless the lane is highly volatile.
- **Full clear**:
  - Provides maximum gold, experience, and tempo advantage. Aim for a full clear when you want to hit level 3 with double buffs, secure the first scuttle, or simply out‑farm the enemy.
  - Adapt the order based on your champion’s clear speed and the enemy’s likely path: start on the side opposite the enemy jungler to avoid early contests, or start on the same side if you want to contest their first buff.
- **Decision factors**:
  - **Champion clear speed**: Slow clearers (Jax, Nasus) must micro‑optimize every auto‑attack and pull to stay on tempo; fast clearers (Shyvana, Graves) can afford slightly more flexible paths.
  - **Game state**: If your lane is losing and you need immediate impact, lean toward a three‑camp gank. If you are ahead or playing a scaling champion, prioritize full clear and tempo.
  - **Objective timers**: Align your clear to hit scuttle spawns (usually at 2:00) or dragon timers; a full clear that finishes just before scuttle lets you contest it without losing tempo.

### Adaptive Pathing & Decision Making
- **React to enemy jungler location**:
  - If you know the enemy is on the opposite side (e.g., you see them top while you are bot), you can safely take your own scuttle, invade their far jungle, or look for a counter‑gank.
  - If they are on your side, play defensively: ward deep, avoid over‑extending, and look for counter‑jungling opportunities when they leave.
- **Objective evaluation**:
  - **Scuttle crab**: Only contest if you have vision, the enemy is elsewhere, or a guaranteed kill on the lane nearby. If you are behind and the enemy has priority, let them take it and look for a gank elsewhere.
  - **Dragon**: Take dragon **only** if you are certain you can finish before the enemy can collapse. Check:
    - Enemy summoner spells (especially flash) on nearby laners.
    - Enemy jungler’s position and distance.
    - Your own smite availability and damage.
    - If unsure, do **not** take it; the risk of losing your jungle for a contested dragon is rarely worth it.
  - **Herald/Rift Herald**: Apply the same logic – only take if you can secure it with vision and a safe escape route.
- **Switching pathing after recalls or kills**:
  - After recalling, immediately check the minimap for enemy jungler position and lane states before deciding your next camp.
  - After a successful kill or assist, assess whether you have time to take a nearby camp, ward, or recall for items before the next camp spawns.
  - If you are low and far from your camps, base to reset, then path from the base toward the objective you need (e.g., base bot side to then walk top for top scuttle).
- **Use of “spare time” for macro plays**:
  - When your camps are down, you can safely:
    - Walk to mid lane to look for a pick or to relieve pressure.
    - Place deep wards in the enemy jungle to track their next move.
    - Attempt a low‑percentage gank if you have vision and the enemy is overextended.
    - Farm a small camp (e.g., wolves) to keep your gold income steady while waiting for a bigger play.

### Common Mistakes
- **Wasting time on low‑percentage plays**: Chasing a dead scuttle, diving a lane with flash up, or invading without vision when you are behind puts you into tempo debt.
- **Ignoring camps‑up vs. camps‑down**: Attempting ganks or invades when your camps are about to respawn (camps up) without a ≥90% chance to succeed.
- **Over‑committing to dragon without vision**: Taking dragon when the enemy can collapse and steal your jungle camps, often resulting in a net loss.
- **Failing to use spare time**: Sitting idle or walking aimlessly after a clear instead of warding, sweeping, or looking for picks.
- **Not tracking the enemy jungler**: Making decisions based on assumptions rather than actual ward information or observed positions, leading to bad invades or missed counter‑ganks.
- **Base for the wrong reasons**: Returning to base when you are not low or when it costs you a camp spawn, breaking your tempo.
- **Rigid route running**: Sticking to a pre‑planned path regardless of enemy movements, lane states, or objective timers.

### Quick Reference
- **Pull and kite every camp** – use auto‑attacks to pull camps toward your next destination to save seconds per clear.
- **Camps up = high‑% only; camps down = free time** – only take ≥90% chance ganks/objectives when your camps will respawn soon; otherwise you can safely scout, ward, or look for low‑percentage plays.
- **Ward enemy jungle to download pathing** – knowing where the enemy jungler is lets you prioritize ganks over scuttle, avoid invades, and time your own objective attempts.
- **Use spare time (20‑30 s after a clear) for vision, lane pressure, or a quick recall** – never sit idle; turn downtime into information or pressure.
- **Take dragon only when you are 100% sure you can finish before the enemy can respond** – if you have any doubt, skip it and look for a play elsewhere.
