## Objective Control

### Dragon Control
- **Secure vision first** – Always have vision on Dragon (or the pit) before committing. Vision lets you see if the enemy is nearby, if they have collapsed, and prevents them from taking your camps uncontested while you’re on the objective.  
- **Use spare time wisely** – After a full clear, use any spare time to ward deep, track the enemy jungler’s position, or reset vision. This informs whether you can safely take Dragon or need to play for side camps instead.  
- **Ask the “can they make it back?” question** – Before smiting Dragon, calculate whether the enemy team can arrive before you finish. Consider:  
  - Current death timers (early game = short timers, late game = longer timers).  
  - Whether you have Smite ready (Smite gives you a secure finish; without it, the window shrinks).  
  - Enemy summoner spells (e.g., no Flash = harder for them to contest or follow up).  
  - Enemy positioning (if their jungler is on the opposite side, they likely can’t collapse quickly).  
- **If unsure, don’t take** – When you cannot confidently answer “yes” to the above, abandon the Dragon attempt. Preserving your side camps and lane pressure is often worth more than a contested Dragon.  
- **Trade side camps for Dragon only when it’s favorable** – Taking Dragon should not cost you your entire jungle side. If the enemy jungler (e.g., Hecarim, Lee Sin) can vacuum your camps while you’re on Dragon, it’s usually a net loss unless you have a clear follow‑up kill or vision advantage.  
- **Leverage flash/no‑flash information** – Knowing an enemy laner (or jungler) lacks Flash creates a kill window. After securing that kill, you can often take Dragon safely before they can respond.  
- **Win the smite fight** – If you have Smite and the enemy does not (or theirs is on cooldown), you can contest Dragon even with equal numbers by smiting it out from under them.  
- **Focus on what’s next** – After taking Dragon (or deciding not to), immediately shift your mindset to the next play: lane pressure, invading the enemy jungle, setting up for the next objective, or resetting to farm. Do not over‑commit to the Dragon pit itself.  
- **Let the enemy waste resources** – If the enemy commits multiple ultimates, teleports, or summoner spells to contest Dragon, you can sometimes disengage, let them waste those cooldowns, then look to steal a buff or catch them out of position.  
- **Later Dragons give more leeway** – As the game progresses, death timers lengthen, making it safer to take Dragon even if the enemy is nearby, because they need more time to regroup. Early Dragons (≈9 min) require a tighter window and stronger vision advantage.

### Baron & Rift Herald (Application of the Same Principles)
- The coaches did not discuss Baron or Rift Herald explicitly, but the decision‑making framework for Dragon transfers directly:  
  1. **Vision** on the pit is mandatory.  
  2. **Calculate enemy response time** (death timers, travel distance, summoner spells).  
  3. **Compare Smite availability** and numbers advantage.  
  4. **Weigh the trade** – taking Baron/Rift Herald should not gift the enemy a free jungle clear or turret plates.  
  5. **Focus on the next play** after securing or denying the objective.  
- Apply the same “can they make it back?” question, using the longer respawn timers of Baron (later game) to your advantage.

### Objective Priority & Setup
- **Priority is situational** – There is no universal “Dragon > Baron” rule; priority depends on:  
  - Your team’s win condition (early game snowball vs. late‑game scaling).  
  - Enemy composition (e.g., heavy engage makes early Dragon riskier).  
  - Current gold/experience leads and map state (tower plates, inhibitor timers).  
- **Setup before the objective** –  
  - Place deep wards in the enemy jungle and river 30‑45 seconds before the objective spawns.  
  - Ping your team to collapse or retreat based on enemy vision.  
  - If you have a lead, consider setting up a slow push in a side lane to force the enemy to choose between defending the objective or losing lane pressure.  
  - Use your spare time after a clear to position yourself for a smite contest or to bait the enemy into over‑committing.

### Trading Objectives for Side Pressure
- **Do not over‑trade** – Taking Dragon/Baron/Rift Herald is only worth it if you retain at least equal map pressure afterward. If the enemy can take two of your camps or a turret while you’re on the pit, the trade is usually negative unless you secure a kill or force a summoner spell.  
- **When behind, prioritize side camps and vision** – If you are behind, giving up an objective to avoid losing your entire jungle is often the correct play; rebuild vision and farm before retrying.  
- **When ahead, you can afford to contest** – A lead gives you the buffer to lose some camps while still coming out ahead if you secure the objective and win the ensuing fight.  
- **Use the objective as a bait** – If you suspect the enemy will over‑commit to Dragon, you can fake a contest, then collapse on them with your own jungler and laners for a pick.

## Common Mistakes
- Taking Dragon without any vision on the pit or river.  
- Ignoring death timers and attempting Dragon when the enemy can arrive before you finish.  
- Over‑committing to Dragon and losing your entire jungle side for little gain.  
- Forgetting to use Smite (or wasting it on a camp) when contesting an objective.  
- Focusing solely on securing the objective and not thinking about the follow‑up play.  
- Not tracking the enemy jungler’s position after a clear, leading to surprise collapses.  
- Disregarding enemy summoner spells (e.g., Flash) when assessing kill windows.  
- Giving up an objective too easily when you actually have a winning fight or vision advantage.  
- Failing to reset vision after an unsuccessful contest, leaving you blind for the next spawn.  
- Using spare time inefficiently (e.g., wandering instead of warding or tracking).

## Quick Reference
- **Vision first** – Ward Dragon/Baron/Rift Herald pits before you commit.  
- **Ask “Can they make it back?”** – Check death timers, Smite, enemy summoner spells, and jungler location.  
- **If unsure, don’t take** – Preserve your side camps and lane pressure over a contested objective.  
- **Use spare time** – Ward, track enemy, and prepare for the next objective or lane state.  
- **Next‑play mindset** – After the objective (taken or denied), immediately shift to the following play: invade, push a lane, or reset to farm.
