## Mental Framework & Decision-Making

### Core Decision Frameworks

#### The What/How/When Concept
- **What**: Identify what you're playing for (objective, lane pressure, win condition) after every significant game event (kill, death, objective, base)
- **How**: Determine execution method (which lanes to push, when to group, vision control needs)
- **When**: Establish timing (specific wave to contest, rotation timing based on enemy respawns)
- Apply this framework consistently - it's the foundation for all mid-game decision making
- Example: After taking Herald, ask "What are we playing for?" (mid tower), "How" (push mid wave first), "When" (on this specific wave)

#### Tempo Management Framework
Tempo = Who can move first on the map and gain man advantage
- **Jungle down Tempo**: Jungler farming/basing → Play safe, don't overextend lanes
- **Objective down Tempo**: After taking objective (need to clear wave/base) → Enemy can move first
- **Base down Tempo**: You're in base while enemy is on map → Communicate danger to team immediately
- **Death Tempo**: Enemy died and respawning → They may have Tempo advantage
- **Key action**: When down Tempo, ping laners to be careful and play safe
- **Key action**: When up Tempo, look for aggressive plays and roaming opportunities
- **Measurement**: Compare death timers + base time to determine Tempo advantage/disadvantage

#### Win Condition Recognition
- Before every major decision, ask: "What is our win condition?"
- Win conditions include: objective control, side lane pressure, mid lane dominance, split pushing
- Align all pathing and decision-making with your team's current win condition
- If win condition is mid lane pressure: Focus there, don't get distracted by side lanes
- If win condition is side lane: Prioritize that lane, avoid unnecessary mid lane presence
- Never force plays that don't serve your win condition (e.g., forcing mid fights when win condition is side lane pressure)

#### Ping Pong Strategy (Pressure Transfer)
- Use winning lane to create pressure that transfers to other lanes for man advantage
- Requirements:
  1. One winning lane that can push wave and move with jungler
  2. Jungler must clear camps first to be available for rotation
  3. Side laner must play safe until jungler arrives
- Execution: Winning lane pushes → creates pressure → side laner rotates → creates man advantage elsewhere
- Most effective when mid lane is winning (faster wave after 14 minutes enables quicker rotations)
- Critical: Side laner awareness - they must know when team is coming through mid lane

### Adapting to Game State

#### Wave State Analysis (Pre-Gank Checklist)
- **Slow push toward enemy**: Gankable (enemy overextended, vulnerable)
- **Even wave**: Standard gank conditions (check vision and enemy positioning)
- **Slow push toward you**: Dangerous (enemy safe under tower, high risk)
- **Fast push toward you**: Enemy vulnerable to dive (enemy pushing into your territory)
- Always check wave state before ganking - it determines enemy positioning and kill potential
- Example: If wave is slow pushing toward enemy, they're likely overextended and gankable

#### Jungler Tracking System
- Track enemy jungler's likely start (wolves/blue vs red/gromp based on invade potential)
- Monitor camp timers: Note when enemy jungler finishes clears to predict gank timing
- Use wave states to anticipate enemy jungler's path (pushing lanes = likely gank direction)
- Know enemy jungler identity: Early game ganker (look for early invades) vs farmer (focus on tracking later)
- Vision confirmation: Place wards at common jungle entrances to verify location
- Critical action: Ping laners when enemy jungler can be on their lane based on your tracking

#### Objective-Based Decision Protocol
- **Before taking objective**: Always ask "What are we playing for after this?"
- **After Baron**: Identify safest tower to take (usually side tower - less collapse risk than mid)
- **After Dragon**: Decide immediately: push lane, take turret, or group for next objective
- **Vision prerequisite**: Establish deep vision control before committing to objectives
- **Exit plan**: Know exactly where to rotate if enemy collapses on your position
- **Baron-specific**: Push mid lane wave first for river control before attempting Baron

#### Base Timers and Itemization Protocol
- Base when: Low HP (<40%), need power spike item, camps are far away (>15s walk), objective about to spawn (<30s)
- **Critical rule**: When basing, immediately think "What am I playing for next?" before leaving base
- **Avoid**: Taking extra camps when you need to base for upcoming objective (loses Tempo)
- **Optimal base timing**: Base on fresh waves (not mid-wave) to maximize pressure upon return
- **Tempo gain**: Properly timed base can give you Tempo advantage by returning before enemy can act

### Mental Approach and Self-Improvement

#### Structured Review Process (The 3 Deaths Review)
1. **Immediately after game**: Review your first three deaths
2. **For each death, analyze three layers**:
   - **Micro**: Spell usage (correct cooldowns?), dodging enemy abilities, positioning
   - **Macro**: Gold spending efficiency, number advantage played with, playing with strongest member
   - **Decision-making**: What were you thinking in-game? Did you anticipate threats? Did you stick to game plan?
3. **Identify 1-2 concrete improvements** per game (not vague "play better")
4. **Focus execution**: Work on those specific improvements in next 5-10 games before adding new ones

#### Champion Identity and Playstyle Adaptation
- **Early game gankers** (Lee Sin, Elise, level 2-3 strong):
  - Look for early opportunities but DON'T force ganks without proper conditions
  - Falling behind on failed ganks is catastrophic - only take high-percentage plays
- **Farming champions** (Lilia, Kindred, level 6 power spikes):
  - Prioritize reaching power spike before forcing aggressive plays
  - Know your weak windows (pre-level 6) and play accordingly
  - Don't over-gank - falling behind defeats your late-game strength
- **Universal rule**: Know your damage output limits for successful ganks
  - Test in practice tool: "Can I kill this laner with my combo + their laner's damage?"
  - If no, don't force the gank - wait for better opportunity or more items

#### Tilt Management and Decision Discipline
- **Stick to your game plan** unless game state changes significantly (objective taken, major kill)
- **Don't chase low-percentage plays** just because you're ahead - preserves leads
- **Recognize when to play safe vs aggressive** based on:
  - Current Tempo (up/down)
  - Win condition alignment
  - Enemy vision and potential counter-play
- **Critical communication**: Ping laners your intentions:
  - "Basing for item X" (prevents overextension)
  - "Rotating to your lane" (enables follow-up)
  - "Enemy jungler here" (enables safe play)
- **Ego check**: Sometimes letting a teammate die is correct if it preserves your win condition
  - Example: If bot lane is lost but your win condition is mid lane pressure, don't throw mid trying to save bot

### Common Mistakes

1. **Ignoring Tempo**: Overextending when down Tempo (jungle/objective/base down), leading to unnecessary deaths and lost objectives
2. **Static win condition**: Continuing to play for outdated objectives after game state changes (e.g., still pushing mid after losing turret when win condition shifted to side lane)
3. **Poor base timing**: Taking "just one more camp" when you need to base for power spike item or upcoming objective (loses Tempo and map pressure)
4. **Force-ganking**: Ganking without proper wave state, vision confirmation, or kill potential (results in death or wasted time)
5. **Jungler blind spots**: Not tracking enemy jungler through wave states, camp timers, and vision (results in surprise invades/counter-ganks)
6. **Baron misuse**: Taking Baron without clear follow-up plan, vision control, or understanding of what to do after (leads to throws)
7. **Over-ganking with farmers**: Falling behind by forcing early ganks with champions that need to scale (Lilia, Kindred)
8. **Communication failure**: Not pinging laners about your intentions (basing, rotating, needing help) leading to misplays
9. **Kill obsession**: Prioritizing kills over objectives, turret plates, and map pressure (loses long-term advantage)
10. **Reactive playing**: Not having a pre-game plan and reacting to events instead of proactively creating advantages

### Quick Reference

1. **Apply What/How/When religiously** after every kill, death, objective, or base - this is your decision-making anchor
2. **When down Tempo**: Immediately play safe, ping laners to be careful, and avoid overextending - your job is to survive until Tempo returns
3. **Post-objective protocol**: Before taking any objective, know exactly what you're playing for after it (tower, lane pressure, next objective)
4. **Ping pong execution**: Only initiate when you have a winning lane that can push - side laner MUST play safe until you arrive for the pressure transfer to work
5. **Base on fresh waves**: Always base when a wave is just clearing (not mid-wave) to maximize pressure and Tempo advantage upon return
6. **Structured self-review**: After every game, review first 3 deaths across micro (mechanics), macro (decision-making), and mental (what you were thinking) - identify 1-2 specific improvements
7. **Win condition fidelity**: Never deviate from your team's win condition without significant game state change - forcing off-condition plays loses games
8. **Jungler tracking trifecta**: Monitor wave states (predict path), camp timers (predict timing), and vision (confirm location) - ping laners when threat exists
9. **Gank discipline**: Only gank when wave state favors you, vision shows enemy position, and you have confirmed kill potential (damage + CC)
10. **Clear communication**: Use pings and short chat to convey your intentions (basing, rotating, needing help) - prevents misplays and enables follow-up
