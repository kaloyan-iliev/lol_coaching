## Champion Matchups & Picks

### Draft and Team Composition Awareness
- Before the game, analyze the draft to understand team strengths and weaknesses
- Identify which lanes are volatile (more volatile = easier to gank)
  - Range vs melee matchups are more volatile (range champions control waves and must play aggressive to get lead)
  - Presence of crowd control increases volatility (makes it easier to kill)
  - Example: Top lane ranged vs melee with CC on melee side = volatile
- Determine your team's swing condition (what your team wants to do)
  - Ask: Do we win 3v2 doing 3v3? Do we win 4v4 on objectives?
  - Consider pre-six vs post-six power spikes
- Know your team composition's win condition
  - Before game: Think about what your team comp wants to do (group, side lane, etc.)
  - If you know what your team condition wants to do, you know if you have to group or play side lane

### Champion Matchup Knowledge
- Lane volatility assessment:
  - Volatile lanes: Range vs melee, presence of CC, lanes where champions must play aggressive
  - Stable lanes: Safe farming lanes where champions can play passive
  - Example: Bot lane with Pike vs Draven/Nami = volatile (will be a "show")
- Matchup-specific considerations:
  - Range vs range matchups: Path towards the lane that has push (whoever is pushing properly has advantage)
  - If both top players have ignite: Definitely best top (volatile, someone takes bad trade and dies)
  - If both top players have teleport: Different considerations (less volatile for invades)
  - Assassins: Don't want to team fight unless good position or team comp strong
    - Prefer sideline picks to get man advantage
    - If lose team fight, cross map
  - Tanks: Better in team fights (front to back)
    - More tanks = better chance to win team fights
- Champion identity and gank potential:
  - Farmers (need level 6): Lower chance of successful ganks pre-6, but can gank if opportunity arises
    - Examples: Kindred, Lillia (need level 6 ideally, but can gank with low success rate pre-6)
    - Philosophy: Don't look for gank unless free kill opportunity arises
  - Street kill (level 2/3 gankers): Higher early gank potential
    - Examples: Most junglers (Lee Sin, Elise, etc.)
    - Key: Don't have to gank if no opportunity; take camps and look for gank later
  - Important: Every jungler should look for gank only if they think it's a free kill
    - Over-ganking when behind loses the game (dying means enemy knows your position)

### Counter-Picking Strategies
- In draft: Hold for champion you want to play (so team can adjust to you)
- Ask teammates what they're playing to measure and adjust your pick
- Break down lanes to identify your real condition:
  - Where do you win 3v2? 3v3?
  - Example: If you're running through mid and airborne is losing, transition mid pressure to bot lane
- Draft considerations for jungle pathing:
  - Range vs range: Path towards pushing lane (whoever has push has advantage)
  - If enemy has strong level 1: Consider starting both sides to avoid invade
  - If enemy can invade level 2 (red side): Ward to see if they do Raptor into your blue
  - If enemy can invade level 3: Start both side, if invaded do your Golems
- Counter-invading:
  - Level 1 invaders: Put ward level one in early game
  - Level 2 invaders (red side): Ward to see Raptor into blue
  - Level 3 invaders: Start both side, if invaded do your Golems
  - General: Put entrance ward, see if coming, start order site if you see them coming

### Team Comp Awareness and Win Conditions
- Recognize what you're playing for (objective + teammate)
  - Always connected to an objective and a teammate
  - Identify which teammate is best to play through and play with
  - Example: If you have Ziggs and their mid lane, you're playing through mid lane
- The "what how when" concept:
  - What: What are you playing for? (objective + teammate)
  - How: How are you playing for it? (which wave, timing)
  - When: When are you playing for it? (specific wave timing)
- After objectives/kills: Immediately think "what am I playing for next?"
  - After kill/death/objective/slash base: Recognize what you're playing for
- Win condition identification:
  - Before game: What is your team's win condition? (Do we win 3v2 doing 3v3?)
  - During game: After getting advantage, what's next objective?
  - Example: After taking Herald, think about what you're playing for next (top tower, Drake, Baron)
- Lane priority and objective control:
  - Mid lane priority is most important after minute 14 (mid wave faster than side lane)
  - First wave you push or mid lane is for control (especially river control)
  - To play for objectives: Secure lane priority first, then go for objective
  - Example: For Baron, secure mid lane push first, then go together
- Tempo management:
